## 1. Registration

- **Act s.18:** no one may act as a data controller or processor unless registered, subject to the thresholds the Data Commissioner sets.
- **Who registers as what (reg 4):** a *data controller* decides the purpose and means of processing. A *data processor* processes on a controller's behalf under contract, with no say over purpose and means. A processor that goes beyond instructions is treated as a controller for that processing.
- **Application (reg 5, 17):** online on the ODPC website, Form DPR1, with establishment documents (e.g. certificate of incorporation), contacts, purposes, categories of data, sensitive data processed, transfers outside Kenya and the safeguards used.
- **Decision:** certificate within 14 days if approved (reg 8). A refusal comes with reasons within 21 days (reg 10).
- **Validity: 24 months** from issue (reg 9). Renew on Form DPR2 with the renewal fee (reg 11).
- **Changes of particulars:** notify the Data Commissioner within **14 days** (reg 15).
- **Offences (reg 18):** processing unregistered, false information, or processing after the certificate expires without renewing. The penalty is under Act s.73.

### Exemption (reg 13)

Exempt from mandatory registration only where the organisation:
- (a) has annual turnover **below KES 5 million** or annual revenue **below KES 5 million**, **and**
- (b) has **fewer than 10 employees**.

"Revenue" is a profit-making organisation's total income for the year before registration. "Turnover" is a non-profit's used annual budget.

The exemption does **not** apply to anyone processing for a Third Schedule purpose. Exempt organisations must still comply with Part IV (principles and obligations) and Part VI (transfers outside Kenya) of the Act.

### Third Schedule (of the Registration Regulations): registration mandatory whatever the size

1. Canvassing political support among the electorate.
2. Crime prevention and prosecution of offenders (including operating a security CCTV system).
3. Gambling.
4. Operating an educational institution.
5. Health administration and provision of patient care.
6. Hospitality industry firms (excludes tour guides).
7. Property management, including selling land.
8. Provision of financial services.
9. Telecommunications network or service providers.
10. Businesses wholly or mainly in direct marketing.
11. Transport services firms (including online passenger-hailing apps).
12. Businesses that process genetic data.

### Fees (Second Schedule)

| Category | Description | Registration (KES) | Renewal (KES, every 2 years) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micro and small | 1–50 employees and turnover/revenue up to KES 5M | 4,000 | 2,000 |
| Medium | 51–99 employees and KES 5,000,001–50M | 16,000 | 9,000 |
| Large | over 99 employees and over KES 50M | 40,000 | 25,000 |
| Public entities | government functions, any size | 4,000 | 2,000 |
| Charities and religious entities | any revenue | 4,000 | 2,000 |

A controller that registers as both controller and processor pays both fees (reg 4(3)). The bands mix employee and revenue tests; where they disagree, ask the ODPC which band applies.

## 2. Principles, lawful basis and consent

- **Principles (s.25):** lawful, fair and transparent; explicit, specified and legitimate purposes; minimal; accurate; kept no longer than needed; a valid explanation whenever family or private affairs are asked about; **not transferred outside Kenya without proof of adequate safeguards or the data subject's consent**.
- **Lawful basis (s.30):** consent for specified purposes, or necessity for: a contract with the data subject; a legal obligation; vital interests; public interest or official authority; legitimate interests (unless unwarranted given the harm to the data subject); or historical, statistical, journalistic, artistic or research purposes. Further processing must fit the original purpose. Processing without a basis is an offence.
- **Consent (s.32):** the controller must be able to prove it. It can be withdrawn at any time, though earlier processing stays lawful. It isn't freely given if a service is made conditional on consent to processing that the service doesn't need.
- **Collect directly (s.28):** from the data subject, with listed exceptions such as public records or consent to collection from another source. Indirect collection: tell the data subject within **14 days** (General Regulations).

## 3. Duty to notify (s.29)

Before collecting, as far as practicable, tell the data subject:
- (a) their rights under s.26;
- (b) that personal data is being collected;
- (c) the purpose;
- (d) third parties it will be transferred to, with the safeguards;
- (e) the controller's or processor's contacts, and whether any other entity may receive the data;
- (f) the technical and organisational security measures;
- (g) whether collection is under a law, and whether it's voluntary or mandatory;
- (h) the consequences of not providing the data.

### Data protection policy (reg 23)

Every controller and processor must **develop, publish and regularly update** a policy on how it handles personal data. This applies whether or not it has to register. The policy may cover:
- the kinds of personal data collected and held;
- how people can access their data and use their rights;
- how complaints are handled;
- the lawful purpose of the processing;
- transfers outside Kenya or to third parties, naming recipients where possible;
- the retention periods and schedule (reg 19);
- collecting children's data, and the criteria used.

A website privacy policy can serve as this policy if it covers these points. The s.29 notice is what people are told at collection; the reg 23 policy is the standing, published account of practice.

## 4. Children (s.33)

- Process a child's data only with a **parent's or guardian's consent**, and in a way that protects and advances the child's rights and best interests.
- Have **age verification and consent mechanisms**, scaled to the technology available, the volume of data, the share likely to be children's, and the possible harm.
- Exception: services exclusively providing counselling or child protection to a child.
- A child is a person under 18 under Kenyan law. Children's data is on the DPIA high-risk list (section 10).

## 5. Sensitive personal data (s.2)

Data revealing a person's race, health status, ethnic social origin, conscience, belief, genetic data, biometric data, **property details, marital status, family details including names of the person's children, parents, spouse or spouses**, sex or sexual orientation.

Sensitive data abroad needs the data subject's consent and confirmed safeguards (s.49). Processing sensitive data is high-risk for DPIA purposes.

## 6. Data subjects' rights and deadlines

Rights (s.26):
- to be informed of how their data will be used;
- to access it;
- to object to all or part of the processing;
- to correct false or misleading data;
- to have false or misleading data deleted.

Also:
- portability (s.38): a structured, machine-readable copy, or a direct transfer where technically possible;
- rectification and erasure without undue delay (s.40), telling third parties who received the data;
- restriction (s.34).

Rights may be exercised by a parent or guardian for a minor (s.27).

Deadlines (General Regulations 2021):

| Request | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Access | 7 days, free of charge (reg 9(4), (6)) |
| Rectification | 14 days. A refusal is notified in writing, with reasons, within 7 days |
| Erasure | respond within 14 days |
| Objection | 14 days, free of charge |
| Restriction | 14 days, free of charge. A refusal is notified, with reasons, within 14 days |
| Portability | 30 days, at reasonable cost (s.38(6)). A refusal is notified, with reasons, within 7 days |
| Stop sharing for a third party's direct marketing | 7 days |
| Indirect collection: tell the data subject | 14 days |

When an objection is refused, the data subject must be told the reasons and their right to complain to the Data Commissioner.

## 7. Commercial use and direct marketing

- **s.37:** using personal data for commercial purposes needs the data subject's **express consent**, or authority under a written law with the data subject told at collection. Anonymise where possible.
- **What counts (reg 14):** using data to induce someone to buy, rent, subscribe to or exchange goods or services. Direct marketing includes catalogues, targeted online adverts to logged-in users, and messages about a sale. Marketing that doesn't use personal data to pick recipients isn't direct.
- **When direct marketing is allowed (reg 15(1)):** only with personal data **other than sensitive personal data**, collected from the person, who was told marketing is a purpose, has consented, has a simple opt-out, and hasn't opted out. Because Kenya's sensitive list is wide, this rules out marketing based on marital status, family details, health, religion, property details and the like, even with consent.
- **Every message (reg 15(2)–(3), 16, 17):** say how to opt out at no charge. Each message carries the opt-out instructions, for example reply STOP to an SMS or an unsubscribe link. Don't disguise the sender, and don't use automated calling without a person. An opt-out must offer stopping all future marketing.
- **Third parties (reg 18):** a person can ask you to stop sharing their data with others for those others' direct marketing. Do it within **7 days**, free of charge.
- **Offence (reg 15(4)):** using personal data for commercial purposes without consent: fine up to **KES 20,000** or up to 6 months' imprisonment, or both.
- The data subject may also object to direct marketing, including profiling for it, and the data must then not be processed for that purpose.

## 8. Retention

- **s.39:** keep data only as long as reasonably necessary for the purpose, unless the law requires or allows longer, it's needed for a lawful purpose, the data subject consents, or it's for research or similar purposes. Then delete, erase, anonymise or pseudonymise it.
- **Reg 19:** keep a **retention schedule** with the purpose, the retention period, and periodic reviews. Erase, delete, anonymise or pseudonymise when the purpose lapses.

## 9. Processors (reg 24)

A controller engages a processor only through a **written contract** covering:
- what is processed, for how long, the nature and purpose, the type of data and the categories of data subjects;
- the controller's obligations, rights and instructions;
- the processor's duties, including confidentiality, security, using sub-processors only with authority, helping with data subjects' rights, deletion or return at the end, and audits.

Read the regulation for the full list when drafting.

## 10. Impact assessments (s.31; General Regulations reg 49–53)

- **When:** before processing that's likely to be **high risk**. The regulations list as high risk:
  - automated decisions with legal or similar effect, including profiling, or using sensitive data to decide access to services;
  - large-scale use of data for a new purpose;
  - biometric or genetic data;
  - changes that raise the risk;
  - **sensitive data, or data about children or vulnerable groups**;
  - combining datasets from different sources and purposes;
  - large-scale processing;
  - **systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area on a large scale** (for example CCTV or facial recognition covering shoppers, passengers or the public);
  - innovative use of new technological or organisational solutions;
  - processing that prevents a data subject from exercising a right.
- **Content (s.31(2)):** a systematic description and purposes; necessity and proportionality; risks to data subjects; measures and safeguards. The regulations have a DPIA template.
- **Submitting the report (s.31(5)):** DPIA reports "shall be submitted sixty days prior to the processing of data". The Act doesn't name the recipient, but read with s.31(3) and reg 51 it is the Data Commissioner. So plan for at least **60 days** between submitting a DPIA and starting processing.
- **Consulting the ODPC (s.31(3); reg 51):** where the DPIA shows high risk, consult the Data Commissioner before processing. The ODPC responds within **60 days**, and may recommend changes. With no reply within 60 days of submission, processing may begin and the report is taken as approved (reg 51(3)).

## 11. Breaches (s.43; reg 37–38)

- **Who must be told:** where data is accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person and there's a **real risk of harm**, notify the **Data Commissioner within 72 hours** of becoming aware, with reasons if late. Tell the affected people in writing within a reasonably practical time, unless they can't be identified. That can be delayed for a criminal investigation.
- **Processors** tell the controller without delay, within **48 hours** where practicable.
- **Notifiable by definition (reg 37):** a breach involving a person's full name or ID number together with data in the regulations' Second Schedule. Or an account identifier (name or number) together with a password, security code, access code, security answer, biometric or other access credential. Publicly available data is excluded.
- **Content:** the nature of the breach, the measures taken, contacts, and enough for people to protect themselves (s.43(5)). Regulation 38 lists what the notice to the ODPC must contain.

## 12. Transfers outside Kenya and local processing

- **s.48:** transfer only with proof to the Data Commissioner of appropriate safeguards (including jurisdictions with comparable laws). Or where it's necessary for: a contract with the data subject; a contract in their interest; public interest; legal claims; vital interests; or compelling legitimate interests not overridden by the data subject's.
- **s.49:** sensitive data out of Kenya needs the data subject's **consent** and confirmed safeguards. The ODPC may ask for proof, or prohibit or suspend transfers.
- **General Regulations Part VII:** transfers on safeguards, adequacy, binding corporate rules, necessity or consent, and onward transfers.
- **Reg 26 (local processing):** these purposes need a server and data centre in Kenya, or at least one serving copy stored in Kenya:
  - civil registration and legal identity;
  - elections;
  - public finance systems;
  - protected computer systems;
  - **early childhood and basic education**;
  - **primary or secondary health care**.

## 13. Data protection officer (s.24)

Optional ("may designate") where processing is by a public or private body, where core activities need regular and systematic monitoring of people, or where core activities involve sensitive data. It can be a staff member without a conflict of interest, or shared within a group. Publish the DPO's contact details on the website.

## 14. Security by design (s.41)

Appropriate technical and organisational measures, both at design time and during processing, with only the data needed for each purpose processed by default. Consider:
- foreseeable risks and safeguards against them;
- pseudonymisation and encryption;
- restoring data after an incident;
- testing that safeguards work.

## 15. Penalties

- **s.63:** the Data Commissioner can fine up to **KES 5 million, or 1% of the previous year's annual turnover, whichever is lower**.
- **s.65:** people who suffer damage, including non-financial damage, can claim compensation from the controller, or from a processor that broke its own duties or its instructions.
- **s.73:** offences with no specific penalty carry a fine up to **KES 3 million**, or up to **10 years'** imprisonment, or both. The court can also order forfeiture or stop a continuing breach.
